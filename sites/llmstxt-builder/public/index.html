What is llms.txt? The 5-minute guide

llms.txt is a plain-markdown file served at the root of your domain (/llms.txt) that gives large language models a curated, noise-free map of your website: what the site is, and which pages matter — each with a one-line summary. Think of it as sitemap.xml for the AI era: sitemaps tell search crawlers where your pages are; llms.txt tells AI systems what they mean.

Why it exists

  • LLM context windows are limited — models can't ingest your whole site, so they need a curated index.
  • HTML is noisy — navigation, ads and scripts bury the actual content. Markdown is what models parse best.
  • AI answers are the new front door: ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews now mediate a growing share of discovery. If models misunderstand your site, they misrepresent it — or skip it.

The format (from llmstxt.org)

# Project name

> One-sentence summary of what this site is.

Optional free-text notes: audience, pricing, anything a model should know.

## Docs
- [Quick start](https://example.com/docs/start): Install and first run in 5 minutes.
- [API reference](https://example.com/docs/api): Every endpoint with examples.

## Optional
- [Blog](https://example.com/blog): Long-form articles; skip unless asked for background.
  • One H1 (#) — the site name. Required.
  • A blockquote (>) — the one-line summary. Strongly recommended.
  • ## sections grouping links, each link as [title](url): description.
  • A section named Optional marks lower-priority links models may skip.
  • Bonus: llms-full.txt — your entire documentation flattened into one markdown file.

Who already uses it

Anthropic, Cloudflare, Stripe, Zapier and thousands of documentation sites publish llms.txt. Frameworks like Docusaurus, VitePress, Mintlify and Nextra can generate one automatically. Adoption is still early — which is exactly why it's an edge.

llms.txt vs robots.txt vs sitemap.xml

  • robots.txt — permissions: who may crawl what.
  • sitemap.xml — inventory: every URL, no meaning.
  • llms.txt — meaning: the few URLs that matter, explained.

You want all three. They answer different questions.
